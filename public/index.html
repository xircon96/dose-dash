If maths makes you anxious

Lots of nursing students feel this way, and practice is the safest place to make mistakes. Use the same four steps every time:

  1. Read the question twice and highlight the numbers.
  2. Write the formula before any numbers.
  3. Work it out one step at a time.
  4. Check it makes sense, then add the unit.

For learning and practice only. In practice, always follow the prescription, local policy and the current BNF or BNFC, and get a second check where required.

Help

Formula sheet

Drug doses (WIG)

Dose = what you Wantwhat you've Got × what it's In

Want 500 mg, got 250 mg in 5 mL: 500 ÷ 250 × 5 = 10 mL. Make the units match first.

Conversions

g to mg to micrograms: × 1000 each step. Back up: ÷ 1000 each step.

1 L = 1000 mL. 1 kg = 1000 g. 1 hour = 60 minutes. 1% w/v = 1 g in 100 mL = 10 mg/mL.

BMI

BMI = weight (kg)height (m)²

Adult ranges: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 healthy, 25 to 29.9 overweight, 30 to 39.9 obese, 40 or more severely obese. NICE advises lower thresholds (overweight from 23, obesity from 27.5) for people of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family background. For under 18s, BMI is plotted on a centile chart.

UK patient-reported measurements

1 stone = 14 lb  ·  1 lb = 16 oz  ·  1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb  ·  1 foot = 12 inches  ·  1 inch = 2.54 cm

Patients and parents often give weight in stones and pounds (or pounds and ounces for babies) and height in feet and inches. Convert to kg and cm or m before any calculation, and record in metric. The exact value is 1 kg = 2.20462 lb; questions say which value to use.

Flow rates

Pump rate (mL/h) = volume (mL)time (hours)
Drops per minute = volume (mL) × drop factortime (minutes)

Standard giving set: 20 drops/mL. Blood giving set: 15 drops/mL. Children's IV fluids always run through a pump. Holliday-Segar maintenance fluids per day: 100 mL/kg for the first 10 kg, 50 mL/kg for the next 10 kg, 20 mL/kg for each kg above 20 kg.

Fluid balance

Balance = total in minus total out

Positive means more in than out. Urine output (mL/kg/h) = urine ÷ weight ÷ hours. A commonly used minimum is 0.5 mL/kg/h for adults and 1 mL/kg/h for children. For nappies, 1 g of extra weight is taken as 1 mL of urine.

Rearranging (missing values)

Volume = rate × time. Time = volume ÷ rate. Weight = BMI × height². Intake = output + balance. Weight = dose ÷ dose per kg.

Using the BNF

The BNF (adults) and BNFC (children) give doses, maximum doses and how often a medicine can be given. For a maximum dose check, find the maximum, add up what has been given, subtract, and make sure the units match. The look-up buttons open a search on the BNF or BNFC website. Reference boxes in questions are simplified for practice, so use the current BNF on placement.

Writing units

Always write the unit. Write "micrograms" in full rather than mcg or µg, and use mL rather than cc. Examples: 2 tablets, 7.5 mL, 125 mL/h, 28 drops/min, 24.7 kg/m², 0.35 mg, 1.5 g, 6 hours.
